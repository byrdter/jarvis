Here's a pattern worth remembering. When 4G arrived, daily life visibly changed: ride-hailing, streaming on the bus, mobile shopping, maps that follow you everywhere. You could point at your phone and say, "this is new."

When 5G arrived, we mostly got... impressive speed tests. By the end of twenty twenty-five, nearly three billion people were expected to be on 5G — but for most of us, daily life didn't feel different. The real changes happened out of sight, in factories and businesses.

The lesson: the headline number is never the real story. SPEED becomes the first candidate on our Impact Map. It matters — but after what 5G taught us, it starts near the outside. It won't be allowed into the center just because it wins a laboratory race.
