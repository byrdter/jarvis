The same radio reflection that catches a grandmother's fall can also log when you're home and when you're not. The same centimetre-level positioning that guides rescue workers to you can track employees more closely than they ever agreed to. The same shared map that coordinates public roads can become a private tollbooth around physical space.

This isn't a reason to reject the technology. But it is a reason to pay attention now, because the decisions get baked in early — what gets sensed, whose consent counts, how long the data is kept, who can buy access, and whether a person without a device can opt out at all. The official roadmaps already list privacy and security as core requirements. Whether that becomes real protection or fine print is being decided before most of us ever hear about it.

This is the final change to our map before the verdict. SENSING and COORDINATION remain closest to us — but now every path to the center passes through consent, access, and control. A capability doesn't become useful merely because it works. It becomes useful when people can trust the rules around it.
