Our Impact Map shows capabilities. It also shows where the work will appear. If you're wondering where the opportunity is, the honest answer is: the work arrives before the magic.

NIST's current research agenda names the fields that need people: wireless measurement, sensing, security, spectrum management, and AI built into networks. Around those sit very practical businesses: installing sensors and connecting them to AI systems, teaching factory robots to use shared maps, testing equipment, keeping spatial data accurate, and certifying that all of it is safe.

These aren't guaranteed job forecasts — nobody can tell you which company wins. But the pattern from every past network generation is the same: the money isn't in "building something with 6G." It's in solving one expensive real-world problem — a port that wastes hours, a highway with too many accidents, a hospital that loses equipment — once connectivity, sensing, and computing finally work together.

That is where our five candidates stop being separate circles. The opportunity lives where REACH, INTELLIGENCE, SENSING, and COORDINATION overlap around a real problem. SPEED supports all of them — but it still doesn't control the map. Something else does: ownership of the measurements.
