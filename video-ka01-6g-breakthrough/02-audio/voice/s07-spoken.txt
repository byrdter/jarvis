Sensing becomes consequential when it feeds COORDINATION.

One vehicle sees only what its own sensors can reach. A connected road could share a hidden hazard. A warehouse could reroute robots around a worker. A port could coordinate vehicles and cranes against one changing map. A hospital could combine equipment location, room activity, and assistance devices.

The framework doesn't promise those finished systems. It does name automated driving, autonomous collaboration between devices for medical assistance, industrial automation, digital twins, and distributed inference.

The pieces aren't yet a universal shared nervous system. But COORDINATION changes the unit of value: from one device receiving information to many machines acting on a common model of physical space.

Now the question is who builds, operates, and governs the spaces around the phone.
