Very-high-frequency signals can carry enormous information, but walls, rain, distance, atmospheric absorption, and imperfect equipment remain. A 6G label doesn't remove those barriers. The Nature team omitted fifty to seventy-five gigahertz from its wireless tests partly because atmospheric absorption is substantial there.

A practical network cannot bet everything on one terahertz beam. In twenty twenty-six, Japan's NICT demonstrated sixty and three hundred gigahertz working together, with beam control and lower-band fallback when the fragile path degraded.

That helps explain why the official 6G framework includes ubiquitous connectivity, remote communities, satellites, agriculture, and disaster response. REACH joins the map. But why does that framework also ask a communications network to locate objects within centimetres?
