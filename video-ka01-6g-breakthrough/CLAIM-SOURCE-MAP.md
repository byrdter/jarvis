## Claims To Reword

| Scene | Unsafe Wording | Safer Wording | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00/02 | “China achieved 206.25 Gbps with this 6G chip” | “Separate Chinese experiments produced several eye-catching results; the 2025 chip paper reports up to 100 Gbps per lane and a 120-Gbps test at 97.5 GHz” | Avoids merging the 2022 370-GHz aggregate with the 2025 device |
| 02 | “A 6G tower now fits on a chip” | “A key signal-processing function was integrated into a tiny photonic chip” | The laboratory system required extensive external hardware |
| 03 | “6G will give everyone 500 Mbps” | “IMT-2030 sets a 300–500-Mbps experienced-rate target in defined scenarios” | Requirement is not deployment performance |
| 04 | “6G will replace phones with glasses” | “Edge-connected spatial devices could become lighter and more useful if the network, compute, and power pieces mature together” | Several dependencies remain unresolved |
| 06 | “6G can see you” | “A 6G network could infer position, motion, or object characteristics from radio signals” | Sensing is not literal vision and varies by implementation |
| 07 | “Cars will see around corners” | “Connected vehicles and infrastructure could share sensed hazards beyond one vehicle's direct line of sight” | Avoids implying omniscience or guaranteed autonomy |
| 10 | “This is what 6G will do” | “This is the most defensible sequence if today's standards targets and demonstrations become deployable systems” | Finale is a forecast, not a product announcement |
