# Visual-First Narrative Beat Map

**Locked title:** *China's 6G Breakthrough Is Real. Faster Phones Are the Least of It*

**Locked thumbnail:** Person inside a network-generated spatial map; **IT CAN SEE**
**Target:** 9:35, faceless, visual-first. This document defines narrative function and screen action, not final VO.

**Motion companion:** `MOTION-ASSET-MAP.md` defines a meaningful screen change every 2–4 seconds, a hard five-second static ceiling, unique real backgrounds, three single-use clips per scene, database-first sourcing, and scene/master render gates.

## The Story Engine

### Whole-runtime question

If China's record-looking 6G advance is not primarily about a faster phone, what part could actually change a person's life—and where would they encounter it first?

### Persistent spine: the 6G Impact Map

A human silhouette remains at the center of five initially locked nodes:

1. **SPEED** — how much information can move?
2. **REACH** — where can the connection remain useful?
3. **INTELLIGENCE** — what can the network decide or compute?
4. **SENSING** — what can radio infer about physical space?
5. **COORDINATION** — what can machines do together because of it?

The map is visible continuously as a subtle corner/minimap or full-screen surface and advances at least every 90 seconds. Before the reversal, SPEED appears closest to the person. At the reversal, the evidence forces the nodes to reorder: SENSING and COORDINATION move inward; SPEED becomes enabling infrastructure.

### Evidence-state key

Every claim about the future carries one visible badge: **DEMONSTRATED**, **STANDARD TARGET**, **PLAUSIBLE**, or **SPECULATIVE**. The badge changes with the evidence; it never decorates the frame.

### Withheld verdict

Do not state before Scene 10 that 6G's most important shift is the network becoming a perception-and-coordination layer. Earlier scenes can discover sensing, but cannot rank it or state the deployment sequence.

## Scene Arc

### S00 — The impossible headline (0:00–0:35, 35s)

- **Narrative job:** Turn apparent confirmation into a question. Establish that the breakthrough is real without surrendering what it means.
- **Screen action:** Black screen receives one live RF trace. Three contradictory evidence objects slam into a split: a viral **206.25 Gb/s** headline; a ruler measuring **11 mm × 1.7 mm** across the chip; a lab link measuring **1.3 m**. The numbers refuse to align. A human silhouette forms behind them and the five locked nodes orbit into place.
- **Payoff now:** This is not imaginary or merely a forecast; real peer-reviewed work exists.
- **New debt:** Why do the most dramatic number, the tiny chip, and the short distance belong to different versions of the story?
- **Evidence:** Nature 2025 + OFC 2022; cream paper crops at exact figures.
- **Mode:** DEN; web/source proof + stat-hero quick montage.
- **Audio/pacing:** Cold metallic hits, three cuts inside ten seconds, then a two-second vacuum as the map locks.

### S01 — What 5G taught us to distrust (0:35–1:20, 45s)

- **Narrative job:** Make the viewer's own experience relevant without declaring 5G a universal failure.
- **Screen action:** A 4G phone transforms consumer life through maps, video, ride services, and app commerce. A 5G speedometer then races ahead while everyday-life cards move more quietly: fixed wireless, crowded venues, private industrial networks. Marketing fireworks fade; a dated evidence ledger remains. SPEED unlocks but stays outside the person.
- **Payoff now:** Generation labels and peak rates do not predict when or where social impact arrives.
- **New debt:** If speed is only one candidate, which capability earns the center of the map?
- **Evidence:** Exact 5G adoption metrics to be selected before VO.
- **Mode:** DEN with a 5-second ATM human breather at the end.
- **Misdirection:** The viewer expects this to become a “5G disappointed us, so 6G will too” argument. It will not.

### S02 — Candidate one: speed (1:20–2:10, 50s)

- **Narrative job:** Give the headline its strongest fair case.
- **Screen action:** A self-drawing optical schematic carries light into the photonic chip. The operating range sweeps **0.5 → 115 GHz**; nine band slots illuminate; test lanes fill to **100 Gb/s**, then the **120-Gb/s at 97.5 GHz** result lands separately. Plausible consumer uses—large spatial models and high-fidelity remote presence—appear only after a PLAUSIBLE badge.
- **Payoff now:** The device's flexibility across bands, not just one raw number, is technically impressive.
- **New debt:** A giant rate in a lab is impressive—but across what distance and with what equipment?
- **Evidence:** Nature figures/tables.
- **Mode:** DEN; self-drawing diagram + data visualization.

### S03 — Candidate two: reach (2:10–3:00, 50s)

- **Narrative job:** Increase the human stake from download speed to whether a connection works where it is needed.
- **Screen action:** The map zooms from city platform to country road to disaster zone. High bands illuminate dense capacity but break against rain, walls, and distance; lower bands and satellite paths route around them. A real NICT 60/300-GHz dual-band demonstration card enters, then the route dynamically falls back. REACH unlocks.
- **Payoff now:** Practical 6G is more likely to be a portfolio of bands and networks than one miraculous terahertz beam.
- **New debt:** More reliable coverage matters, but it still does not explain why the standard wants the network to know where things are.
- **Evidence:** ITU/3GPP requirements + NICT demonstration.
- **Mode:** DEN; schematic map + documentary environments + source card.

### S04 — Candidate three: intelligence (3:00–4:10, 70s)

- **Narrative job:** Move from transporting information to deciding where information and compute should go.
- **Screen action:** A person wearing ordinary-looking lightweight glasses enters a station. Tasks split between device, edge, and cloud; a latency/energy router redistributes them. Cut to a field technician receiving a spatial overlay and a small firm renting local inference only when needed. INTELLIGENCE unlocks, but three dependency locks—battery, economics, coverage—remain visible.
- **Payoff now:** AI-native networking could make computing feel more ambient and devices less self-contained.
- **New debt:** Smarter routing is still a network moving data. Why does the standards map contain sensing and centimetre positioning?
- **Evidence:** ITU capability targets; device examples labeled PLAUSIBLE.
- **Mode:** DEN; generated/pixel B-roll + analysis HUD + diagram.
- **Spine escalation:** Three nodes are open, but none explains the thumbnail.

### S05 — Reversal: unfold the laboratory (4:10–4:55, 45s; 44–52%)

- **Narrative job:** Destroy the “tiny chip equals finished 6G system” interpretation and change the meaning of the question.
- **Screen action:** Hold the chip alone for one beat. The camera pulls back as every omitted component materializes: external lasers, EDFAs, photodetectors, electrical amplifiers, horn antennas, and the **1.3 m** path. The 2022 **103.125-Gb/s net at 370 GHz** paper separates from the 2025 chip paper; the viral **206.25** label is stamped **AGGREGATED / SEPARATE EXPERIMENT**. SPEED fractures out of the center. The map rotates, revealing two hidden inner tracks leading toward SENSING and COORDINATION.
- **Payoff now:** The chip solves a difficult signal-generation/processing problem. It does not shrink an entire base station onto 19 square millimetres, and it does not repeal propagation.
- **New debt:** If that does not make the breakthrough trivial, what larger system role does flexible radio processing make possible?
- **Evidence:** Nature apparatus and method + OFC abstract.
- **Mode:** DEN; cream evidence register unfolding into dark analysis schematic.
- **Authored reversal:** The question changes from “How fast can 6G be?” to “What can a network become?”

### S06 — Candidate four: sensing (4:55–5:55, 60s)

- **Narrative job:** Pay the thumbnail promise while opening a larger moral question.
- **Screen action:** Return to the thumbnail person. No camera is present. Radio paths reflect from the person, furniture, and walls; an inference layer resolves position, movement, and a fall event. A ruler collapses from metres to the ITU **1–10 cm** target. The SENSING node unlocks and moves closest to the person. On-screen wording changes from **SEE** to **INFER**.
- **Payoff now:** “It can see” means radio infrastructure could infer aspects of physical space while communicating—not literal optical vision.
- **New debt:** Who controls a sensing layer that may work even when the person is not actively using a camera or app?
- **Evidence:** ITU sensing/positioning targets + CityUHK ISAC mechanism; fall example remains PLAUSIBLE pending direct source.
- **Mode:** DEN; self-drawing RF diagram + generated spatial-map shot.

### S07 — Candidate five: coordination (5:55–6:55, 60s)

- **Narrative job:** Escalate from an individual's device to systems acting on shared physical information.
- **Screen action:** Four ghosted environments resolve one at a time: vehicle and roadside unit share an obscured hazard; warehouse robots reroute around a person; a port coordinates cranes and autonomous carriers; hospital equipment locates a critical item and detects room occupancy. Signal paths become a common world model. COORDINATION unlocks and connects all five nodes.
- **Payoff now:** The consequential capability may be many machines acting from a shared, rapidly updated picture—not one phone receiving a faster stream.
- **New debt:** Which of these arrives first, and who gains or loses when physical space becomes computable?
- **Evidence:** ITU use cases plus application-specific sources still to be selected; each vignette carries its badge.
- **Mode:** DEN; four-way numbered grid + pixel/documentary B-roll + constellation hub.

### S08 — The opportunity ladder (6:55–7:45, 50s)

- **Narrative job:** Translate the system into businesses, jobs, and choices without turning the episode into an investment pitch.
- **Screen action:** A vertical ladder builds from infrastructure to services: RF/photonics integration → edge orchestration → spatial-data systems → robotics integration → safety/privacy/compliance. Each rung divides into **NEW WORK**, **CHANGED WORK**, and **AUTOMATED TASKS**. The human silhouette moves from consumer at the bottom to operator/owner at the top.
- **Payoff now:** Near-term opportunity is likely to cluster around integrating and governing systems in controlled environments, not building a generic “6G app.”
- **New debt:** The same layer that creates useful spatial intelligence creates a valuable record of human activity. Who owns it?
- **Evidence:** Mechanism-grounded inference; validate with labor/investment evidence before final VO.
- **Mode:** DEN; data-viz ladder + dossier rows.

### S09 — The price of an aware network (7:45–8:35, 50s)

- **Narrative job:** Compound the stake and prevent technological triumphalism.
- **Screen action:** The spatial map produces three forks: **SAFETY / SURVEILLANCE**, **ACCESS / EXCLUSION**, **SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE / PRIVATE CONTROL**. A fall-alert benefit and an invisible occupancy log occupy opposite sides of the same radio reflection. The viewer receives a five-second held shot of an ordinary room with only the mesh moving.
- **Payoff now:** The social choice is not simply whether sensing works; it is consent, retention, security, access, and control.
- **New debt:** Given the technical limits and these tradeoffs, what is the defensible order in which real people will feel 6G?
- **Evidence:** ISAC mechanism plus privacy/governance sources to add before VO.
- **Mode:** DEN with one held pacing breath; comparison split + minimal live bed.

### S10 — Verdict: the order of arrival (8:35–9:25, 50s)

- **Narrative job:** Answer the opening question once, plainly, then turn it into a useful forecast.
- **Screen action:** The circular Impact Map becomes a dated horizon: **FIRST — reliability and hybrid coverage**; **NEXT — sensing and machine coordination in factories, ports, transport, campuses, and hospitals**; **LATER — ambient consumer spatial experiences if cost, power, privacy, and standards align**. SPEED becomes the foundation under the timeline rather than its destination. The person remains at the center, now surrounded by explicit control toggles for consent and data ownership.
- **Verdict:** The breakthrough's most important possibility is not a faster phone. It is communications infrastructure becoming a perception-and-coordination layer—and the first meaningful effects are likelier to arrive around people, in infrastructure and controlled workplaces, before they arrive as a spectacular feature in their hands.
- **Evidence:** Synthesis from Nature, ITU, 3GPP, NICT, and ISAC work; label the sequence **OUR EVIDENCE-BASED FORECAST**.
- **Mode:** DEN; timeline/data visualization + landing card.
- **Close:** Give one practical viewer test: whenever a 6G claim appears, ask whether it is demonstrated, a standards target, plausible, or speculative.

### S11 — Post-verdict CTA (9:25–9:35, 10s)

- **Narrative job:** Invite return only after delivering the promised answer.
- **Screen action:** The four evidence-state badges collapse into the KeyAdvances mark; end-screen space opens.
- **Function, not copy:** One sentence promising more evidence-ranked near-future developments; no new claim or unresolved tease.
- **Mode:** DEN landing; CTA after verdict.

## Withholding Ledger

| Scene | What the viewer NOW KNOWS | What they still CAN'T ANSWER |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Real experiments produced a tiny chip, huge rates, and a very short link—but the viral story combines them badly | Which fact matters to life, and why the pieces do not line up |
| 01 | Peak speed and generation labels are weak predictors of lived impact | Whether 6G's meaningful difference is speed, reach, intelligence, sensing, or coordination |
| 02 | The chip is genuinely broad-band and fast under laboratory conditions | Whether that translates into distance, deployability, or a human-scale change |
| 03 | Practical systems will mix bands and networks; reliability may matter more than one peak | Why positioning and sensing are native requirements, not side features |
| 04 | AI-native networking could redistribute computation and enable lighter interfaces | What makes this fundamentally different from a smarter 5G network |
| 05 | The tiny chip was part of a large lab system; speed is an enabler, not a self-sufficient revolution | What new role the network can perform once flexible processing, AI, and positioning converge |
| 06 | Radio networks may infer space, motion, and position while communicating | Whether sensing or coordination is the main change, and who controls ambient inference |
| 07 | Shared physical information could coordinate machines beyond one device's view | Which effects arrive first and whether value accrues to ordinary people, operators, or owners |
| 08 | Integration and governance may create new work while altering existing tasks | Whether those opportunities outweigh surveillance, inequality, and control risks |
| 09 | Benefit and surveillance can be two uses of the same signal | The defensible order of arrival and the final meaning of the breakthrough |
| 10 | The likely sequence and final verdict | Resolved |
| 11 | The channel will use the same evidence-state test on future advances | Resolved; no new narrative debt |

## Retention and Integrity Gates

- First verdict phrase: Scene 10, approximately 89% of runtime.
- Reversal: Scene 05, approximately 44–52%.
- Longest visual/VO spine gap: under 90 seconds by design; the map advances in every scene.
- CTA: after the verdict, 10 seconds maximum.
- Honest misdirections: `206.25` number, tiny-chip scale, speed-as-answer, sensing-as-pure-convenience. Each is corrected with primary evidence.
- No scene merely adds support: every row pays a fact and makes the unresolved question harder or more consequential.
- Atmosphere budget: approximately 10 seconds total, under 2% of runtime.
- No static interval reaches five seconds; plan to 2–4-second meaningful events and verify the rendered scenes plus assembled master.
- Every scene carries a unique real background and at least three single-use clips, with the detailed sequence in `MOTION-ASSET-MAP.md`.
