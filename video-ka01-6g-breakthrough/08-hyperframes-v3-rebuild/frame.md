# V3 Frame and Motion Direction

The picture is footage-led editorial technology, not a slide deck. The base layer is always a relevant photograph or clip. Information is attached to the thing it describes through measurement marks, moving paths, progress instruments, source strips, silhouettes, maps, and spatial annotations.

## Palette and type

- Deep navy scrim: `#06121d`
- Warm white: `#f4f1e8`
- Signal cyan: `#66e0e5`
- Evidence amber: `#f0b84b`
- Warning coral: `#ef685f`
- Headline face: Archivo Black / Arial Black fallback
- Instrument face: IBM Plex Mono / monospace fallback

## Composition rules

- Footage remains recognizable; scrims are directional and local, not a dark blanket.
- Text labels stay short and bind to an object, path, gauge, or source artifact.
- Camera movement is continuous but restrained: 2–5% scale travel, short directional pushes, and motivated reframes.
- Transitions use shared motion, light wipes, match cuts, and foreground occlusion. No visible dead gap and no generic fade-to-black.
- A small five-notch spine instrument may persist at the screen edge, but it only illuminates one active contender at a time.
- Source evidence arrives after the hook has earned attention and appears as a physical crop or citation strip, not a page of prose.

## Motion signatures for the opening

- 200 Gb/s: count-up plus a movie download ring that completes on “about a second.”
- Fingernail chip: measurement brackets close around the chip while the camera pushes in.
- Four feet: an SVG beam and ruler draw between real horn antennas.
- Three claims: three artifacts separate in depth and the false connective line snaps.
- Better question: the phone-speed instrument is physically pushed aside by a human silhouette.
- Five candidates: each contender appears as a different visual behavior over relevant footage, then collapses into the edge instrument.
