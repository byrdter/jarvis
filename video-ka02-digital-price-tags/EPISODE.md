## Thesis

One-sentence thesis: Electronic shelf labels are a real operational advance and not evidence of surge
pricing by themselves; the consequential change is that shelf prices have become cheap and fast to
execute, so consumer protection must govern the price-setting and customer-data systems upstream of
the display.

## Audience

- Primary viewer: an ordinary shopper who has seen, or will soon see, a digital shelf label.
- What they already know: grocery prices feel unstable and digital tags can change quickly.
- What they should understand by the end: the label, the pricing system, and the customer-data system
  are three different layers; current evidence supports both real benefits and a need for guardrails.
- Why this matters now: Walmart says about 2,300 U.S. locations already use the labels and expects a
  chain-wide system within a year, while states and cities are actively legislating around the risk.

## Runtime
