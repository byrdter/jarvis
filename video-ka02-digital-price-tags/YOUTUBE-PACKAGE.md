## Pre-Script Packaging Gate

- Channel profile: KeyAdvances
- Concrete viewer payoff: understand what digital shelf labels do today, what they make easier
  tomorrow, and which rules would protect shoppers without banning the useful display technology.
- Carried question / unresolved contradiction: If the label does not set the price, what changed—and
  what actually stops the systems behind it from changing prices more often or differently by shopper?
- Recommended working title: `Your Grocery Store Can Change Prices in Minutes. What Stops It?`
- Recommended thumbnail direction: one unmistakable electronic grocery label caught mid-refresh,
  with a human approval icon on one side and an algorithm/data pattern on the other; `WHO DECIDES?`
- Title/thumbnail division of labor: title establishes the new capability and missing restraint;
  thumbnail asks who has agency. Neither claims current Walmart surge or personalized pricing.
- Gate status: approved

## Title Options

| Option | Title | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — recommended | `Your Grocery Store Can Change Prices in Minutes. What Stops It?` | Proven speed, direct human relevance, and a truthful unresolved question |
| 2 | `Walmart's New Price Tags Aren't the Part to Fear` | Strong reversal; narrower and slightly more declarative |
| 3 | `The Grocery Price Tag Just Became Programmable` | Memorable threshold language; less explicit human payoff |

Rejected earlier wording: `Your Grocery Store Can Change Every Price in Minutes. Will It?` Walmart
supports days-to-minutes execution for large price-change workloads, but not the literal claim that
all prices in a store are changed in minutes. Removing “every” makes the promise exact.

## Thumbnail Brief

- Core visual: macro photograph of a real electronic shelf label during a cyan refresh/glitch split;
  a small human approval symbol on the warm side and a data/algorithm trace on the cool side.
- Face/person included: no; a shopper's hand may enter frame for scale.
- Text on thumbnail: `WHO DECIDES?`
- Background: softly defocused grocery aisle, darkened enough for mobile legibility.
- Color emphasis: warm amber for the physical shelf/human decision; restrained cyan for the digital path.
- Source assets: rights-cleared real ESL close-up and grocery aisle; no synthetic product or fake price.
- Prompt / design notes: one focal object, no red arrows, no surveillance camera collage, no fake
  personalized prices, no tiny interface elements. Test at 10% size.
- How it complements rather than repeats the title: the title asks what constrains fast changes; the
  image asks which actor controls them.

## Description

```text
Digital shelf labels can turn a price change that once took days into a task completed in minutes.
That is a real improvement for store workers—and a capability shoppers do not automatically trust.

We separate the electronic label from the systems behind it, examine what retailers are doing now,
and follow the evidence to the question that matters: what should govern a price once changing it is
almost frictionless?
```
