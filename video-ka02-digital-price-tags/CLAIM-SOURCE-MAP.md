## Source Notes

- “No surge pricing” is the reversal, but never turn one working paper at one chain into “ESLs can
  never cause surge pricing.”
- “The label does not set the price” is technically important; the connected price engine does.
- Treat all retailer, vendor, union, advocate, and industry statements as attributed positions.
- Consumer Reports' online experiment is the strongest documented grocery example, but it is not an
  in-store Walmart ESL example and must never be edited to imply that it is.

## Claims To Reword

| Scene | Current Wording | Safer Wording | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Walmart can change every price in minutes | Walmart says large price-change workloads that took days can now be completed in minutes | “Every” is not established |
| 04 | Digital labels enable personalized prices | Digital labels remove one execution barrier; individualized prices require additional data and delivery systems | Label alone cannot personalize |
| 06 | The study proves there is no surge pricing | The study found virtually no adoption-driven surge pricing at the chain it examined | Scope and working-paper limit |
| 07 | Instacart used personal data to charge shoppers more | Instacart tested different prices across randomized customer cohorts; CR found no demographic targeting evidence | Personal-data claim not established |
| 09 | States are banning digital labels | New Jersey paused new use for one year; other cited measures are proposals | Avoid collapsing law and proposals |
| 11 | These rules will prevent price abuse | These rules would make rapid or individualized changes more visible and constrained | Policy outcome is prospective |
