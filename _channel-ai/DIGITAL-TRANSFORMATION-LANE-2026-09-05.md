## The sharpest finding — the scholarship is the problem, not the translation

You proposed translating scholarly research into the AI world. **The translation is welcome. The
scholarly framing is what kills it.**

| Credential framing | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **"a professor explains what is really happening"** | **DEAD — 0.01× median** |
| "researchers studied this for twenty years" | INCONCLUSIVE, n=2 (rows are mummy DNA) |
| "we have known this since the 1990s" | INCONCLUSIVE, n=1 |
| "the study that predicted all of this" | 78% drift — rows are GCSE exam predictions |
| *(2026-09-04)* "i have worked in tech for thirty years" | 0.01× median |
| **"an engineer explains why this is wrong"** | **PROVEN — 11.51× on 15,000 subs, 35.3 min** |

**Practitioner credential travels. Academic credential does not.** Three independent measurements
now agree. Lead with competence — *what you can work out* — never with authority — *what you know
and where you learned it.*

This is the single most actionable thing in this report, and it is the exact opposite of how a
research background instinctively wants to present itself.

## What the DT literature IS worth — two things, both already proven

The framing does not open a new lane. It converges straight back onto instruments already measured.

### 1. Ammunition for the arithmetic-debunk (Shape 1)

The literature made **falsifiable claims with dates on them.** Nine Elements is 2014, updated 2021.
It is 2026. Those predictions can now be scored — and scoring an aging expert claim is the strongest
shape measured all week (23.73× on 13,100 subs; 19.33× on 14,400).

The related probe supports it, with a caveat:

- *"what the experts got wrong about technology"* — 4 hits, but drift-downgraded. **Read the rows:**
  **19.97× on 250,000 subs — 4.99M views, 75.2 min** (*"What Everyone Gets Wrong About AI and
  Learning"*) · **12.00× on 15,000 subs, 35.3 min** (*"Can 13,000 engineers really be wrong?"*) ·
  6.42× on 6,350 · 1.52× on 2,530 at 72.7 min. Runtimes of **27, 35, 72 and 75 minutes.**

That top row is worth sitting with: **the AI-and-learning question, at 75 minutes, did five million
views** — the same subject the Ng interview raised yesterday.

**But the generic prediction-scoring framings are dead**, so do not title it that way:
*"the technology predictions that failed"* → DEAD 0.08×. *"the future we were promised"* → PROVEN at
0% drift, but the rows are **Frutiger Aero nostalgia aesthetics** (46.71× is a vaporwave-adjacent
illustration video); the one genuine analytical row is Amy Shira Teitel's Artemis II piece, 3.47× on
74,100 subs at 41.1 min. That is an aesthetic lane, not an analysis lane.

### 2. Taxonomy structure — the atlas

The Nine Elements framework's real gift is not its content but its **shape**: three domains, nine
components, an organizing structure imposed on a chaotic subject. That is an atlas, and the atlas
measured strongly — Leo Cui's *"The Entire AI Data Center Explained"* at **13.05× on 31,600 subs,
40.3 min**, and the Ordinary Economics work found atlas beating narrow instrument by 18–110× within
the same channel.

**A nine-element taxonomy applied to AI is a format you already know how to build.** Just never call
it digital transformation.

## On your premise about AI

> *"AI itself does not seem to have much going for it unless you are doing news or controversy and
> arguing about the same old three or four things."*

**Half right, and the half that is wrong matters.**

You are right that the proven subject set is narrow — jobs, the bubble, slop, data centers,
cognition. Five things.

You are wrong that it needs news or controversy. The strongest shape measured all week is
**arithmetic** — a credentialed practitioner running the numbers on a claim — and it is neither. It
is measurement. *"The Math Behind 'AI Will Replace Engineers' Is Embarrassing"* did 310,926 views on
a **13,100-sub** channel at 54 minutes with no news peg and no controversy, just a calculation.

**The answer to a narrow subject set is not a broader subject. It is a deeper instrument on one of
them** — which is exactly what a research background buys you, and exactly what nobody else in the
AI-commentary space is doing.

## What I would actually do

1. **Drop "digital transformation" as a name, a topic and a frame.** 0.02× at 0% drift.
2. **Keep the literature as a target list.** Every dated, falsifiable claim in it is a Shape 1 video.
3. **Present as a practitioner, never as a scholar.** "An engineer runs the numbers," not "the
   research shows." 11.51× versus 0.01×.
4. **Use DT frameworks for structure, not subject** — the taxonomy is the atlas format.
5. **Nearest concrete build, unchanged from yesterday:** the AI-and-learning arithmetic, in the
   proven brain/cognition lane, at 40–75 minutes. The 4.99M-view row says the ceiling is high; the
   Ng paradox is the hook; the underlying studies still need pulling and reading.

**Not probed, worth a round when you want it:** the everyday-life objects reframed as *archaeology*
rather than *grievance* — the fax machine and the British app result both suggest that framing works
where complaint does not.
