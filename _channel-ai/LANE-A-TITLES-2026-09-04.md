**The pattern:** document-forensics framing does not sell. *Doing* the forensics is the method;
*advertising* the forensics is a dead title. Say what you found, never how you found it.

### The most useful negative result in the scan

**"95 percent of AI projects fail" → DEAD.** 5% drift, 0.03× median, seven on-topic rows, every one
of them flat. That is the most-cited AI statistic of the year and six separate channels made a video
about it to no audience at all.

Why it matters: **debunking a pessimistic statistic is not the same as debunking an optimistic
promise.** The audience already believes AI projects fail. There is no gap to open. Shape 1 works
because it attacks a claim people still *believe* — "AI will replace engineers" — not one they
already suspect. Pick targets accordingly: **the ripe target is always the claim still being sold.**

   the residency and employment counts are not verified and must be pulled before they go on screen.

   *Your specific claim* inside it is not. Re-probe if the target narrows the proven lane.
3. **Runtime comes from rendered audio, never a word count.** The measured error was 82 seconds on a
   17-minute script. Shape 1 wants 30–50 min, so the 8:00 floor isn't at risk — but the number still
   has to come from the render.
