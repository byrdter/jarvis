## 1. AI and learning — yes, but not in the words you used

You asked specifically about this, so: **the topic is live, the framing isn't.**

The direct research framings return nothing (*"students who use ai remember less"* → n=0;
*"ai is bad for learning"* → no on-topic hit above 1.0×). But the adjacent lanes were already
measured PROVEN earlier this week:

| Lane | Verdict | Best reachable rows |
|---|---|---|
| what ai is doing to your brain | **PROVEN** | **4.75× on 253,000 subs — 1.20M views** · 2.19× on 121,000 (264k views) |

| ai in schools cheating | **PROVEN** | 2.94× on 33,900 · 1.88× on 15,400 |

**So: don't title it "AI is bad for learning." Title it into the brain/cognition lane, which is
proven, and use Ng's material as the evidence inside it.**

### And what Ng gives you there is genuinely strong

He hands you a **paradox with a credentialed source on the unexpected side** — the §3 hook ideal:

> The man who co-founded Coursera, built Google Brain, taught roughly **8 million people** AI, and
> has just taken **$100M from Coursera** to lead a new AI-learning company (LearnVector) says on
> camera: *"frankly AI models are terrible for learning."* He flags it himself as controversial —
> *"I don't know I've said this publicly."*

And he grounds it in a shape that is *itself* Shape 1 arithmetic:

- Students score **higher on homework** when they use AI. Their **long-term retention is much worse.**
- The mechanism he names is **cognitive offloading** — and he indicts himself with it: he asked a
  model how a front-end/back-end component worked, got the answer, and six months later had to ask
  again because he'd retained nothing.
- He does not let his 5- and 7-year-old use a calculator for multiplication, for exactly this reason.

That is a real research literature you can go and read — **and it needs verifying against the papers,
not against Ng's summary of them.** He says "the data is very clear" and "more and more studies are
coming out"; he names none of them on camera.

## 2. The strongest takeable — the 30/60 arithmetic

This is the one that lands directly in an already-PROVEN shape (*"the math behind the AI claim is
wrong"* — 23.73× on 13,100 subs, 19.33× on 14,400).

Ng's actual argument, verbatim in substance:

- AI might do **30–40% of many jobs**. He credits the task-decomposition work of **Erik Brynjolfsson
  (Stanford)** and **Andy McAfee (MIT)** — named sources, checkable.
- Therefore the remaining **60%** becomes *more* valuable, because it is an **economic complement**
  to the part that just got cheaper.
- The counterintuitive evidence: software engineering is the profession **most** affected by AI, and
  **job openings in it are up**.

**Frame it at the companies, not at the worried viewer.** The video is not "you'll be fine." It is
*"the people selling you the 50%-of-jobs number are the people selling the product"* — which is
Shape 3, measured PROVEN at 9.27× on a 5,460-sub channel.

## 3. The regulatory-capture thesis — real, but retitle it

Ng makes a specific, named, mechanical accusation:

> A handful of leading AI companies have been *"very loud voices of fear-mongering around AI to try
> to get regulations passed to create an unfair playing field that favors incumbents, so that we all
> have to pay a high toll for use of AI"* — because if you have spent billions training a model, it
> is *"really inconvenient if someone else trains a model and wants to give it to anyone in the
> world to use for free."*

He calls the AI-as-nuclear-weapons analogy one that *"has no basis in fact."*

**As a title, "AI fear is manufactured" is dead (0.07×). As "companies are lying about AI" it is
PROVEN.** Identical content. The difference is whether the sentence has a defendant.

## 4. ⚠️ A collision you should know about

Ng says, in the same breath as the fear-mongering charge, that people *"spread misinformation about
how AI uses data centers, uses a lot more water than the actual reality."*

**Our strongest measured lane is data centers**, and part of what travels in it is exactly the
water-and-power alarm. That is not a reason to drop the lane — it is a reason to be careful, and it
is arguably a gift:

- The proven Shape-1 instrument in that lane is already *"Process Engineer Explains: The Math Behind
  'Water-Efficient AI Data Centers'"* (1.72×) and *"You're being lied to about electricity and AI
  data centers"* (15.29× on 2,420 subs).
- So the arithmetic-debunk of the water claim is **a live sub-lane with a credentialed contrarian
  now on record.** Whichever way the numbers actually fall, running them is the video.

**Do not take Ng's word for the water figures either.** He is an interested party — he says so about
everyone else. Pull the utility filings.
