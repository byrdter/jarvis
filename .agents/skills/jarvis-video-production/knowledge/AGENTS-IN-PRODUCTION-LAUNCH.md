## 4. Footage: the 50/50 split — but not the 50/50 you proposed

**Agreed on the principle, with one important correction to what "real footage" means here.**

The "84–97% real material" constant in `reference_proven_doc_channels_presentation` came from
**documentary** channels — Modern MBA, WSM, Explorist. That genre needs archival and stock because its
subject is out in the world.

**Fireship — the actual model for this channel — is not 84–97% licensed footage.** Frame inspection of
its DeepSeek video showed real *captures* (the actual benchmark chart, Altman's real tweet, the NVDA
chart mid-crash, Bloomberg, Reddit), memes, and its own graphics. Almost no stock b-roll at all.

> **For an engineering channel, "real footage" means YOUR OWN SCREEN.** The terminal. The actual
> Claude Code session. The real error. The dashboard. The token count ticking up. The `~/.claude`
> directory. Real GitHub issues, real docs pages, real pricing pages.

That is better than stock on every axis: it is free, it is impossible for a competitor to copy, and
**it is the proof layer that licenses every claim the channel makes.** Stock footage of someone typing
in an office would actively weaken the moat — it says "generic explainer."

**So the split I would run:**

| share | material | job |
|---|---|---|
| **~50%** | **Real screen capture** — your terminal, real sessions, real failures, real telemetry, real docs/pricing pages | **proof.** This is the moat, on screen. |
| **~40%** | **HyperFrames** — architecture diagrams, flow, comparison splits, scale/count-ups, timelines | **explanation.** Things a screen recording cannot show. |
| **~10%** | Generated stills / light meme punctuation | **pace and levity.** Fireship uses these as punctuation, never as evidence. |

**Do not abandon HyperFrames** — an agent architecture, a context window filling up, a subagent fan-out
are exactly the things no camera can film and HyperFrames does well. The rule is simply:
**HyperFrames explains; screen capture proves.** Never let HyperFrames stand in for evidence you could
have shown for real.

Existing standards still apply: motion floor and density gates (`motion-scan.py`), finding type ≥60px
(`feedback_finding_type_is_large`), no naked text.

## 6. Honest limits

- **This fixes discovery, not retention.** Every teardown in this corpus measures the click. The
  channel was independently diagnosed as retention-gated; better titles get people in the door.
- **The head terms are genuinely crowded** (competition 50–69). The recommendation rests entirely on
  the operator tail (26–43) and the telemetry moat. If a large channel targets the same tail, the
  advantage is the moat, not the keywords.
- **Keyword numbers are vidIQ estimates.** Cluster shape is clear; individual figures are directional.
- **Re-titling published videos does not re-run their browse test** — but search ranking is not gated
  the same way, so they can accrue traffic over months. Expect slow compounding, not a spike.
