## The findings

**1. Distribution is NOT the problem. YouTube served 22,750 impressions to a 14-sub channel.**
This is the exact opposite of the main channel's §0 diagnosis (≈0% Browse/Search). Browse features was
74.7% and 82.6% on the two videos checked in detail; ~95–97% of impressions came from YouTube
recommending the content. **The algorithm went looking for an audience, repeatedly.** Every hour spent
this session hunting for demand was solving a problem we did not have.

**2. The wall is CTR. Channel-wide 2.3%.** Healthy is roughly 4–6%. We are converting impressions at
about half the rate we need — and we have the impressions.

**3. The single most instructive row: `Will AI Agents Change Everything?` — 2,114 impressions,
0.5% CTR, 13 views.** YouTube showed it to over two thousand people and **1 in 200 clicked**. There is
no topic problem, no retention problem, no runtime problem in that number. It is a title problem, in
isolation, measured.

**4. Within the reliable set (>1,000 impressions) the spread is 7×:**

| CTR | Title shape |
|---|---|
| **3.5%** | We Tested **[named thing]**'s Claims — Here's What's **Actually True** |
| 2.7% | The End of Billionaires**?** |
| 2.3% | Long Context AI: What **2 Million Tokens** Actually Means |
| 1.7% | AI Agents Destroying Office Jobs?? |
| 1.6% | Google Just Made **Chrome** Autonomous — Here's What That Really Means |
| 1.5% | MIT's 2026 Breakthrough Technologies |
| 1.3% | The Robot Revolution in Factories |
| **0.5%** | Will **AI Agents** Change Everything? |

**Named, specific, checkable thing + a verdict → top. Abstract category + vague question → bottom.**
Independently the same finding the demand probes produced (verdict-led proves, mechanism/abstract
dies) — arrived at from our own audience.

**5. `Apple Just Spent $110 Billion — Genius Move or Corporate Blunder?` is the outlier worth study:
7.7% CTR and 4 subscribers — the most subs of anything on the channel** — on only 495 impressions.
It is the most *specific* title we have written: named company, named number, binary verdict. Sample
is modest (~38 clicks) so treat as strong-suggestive, not proven. **It is also not about AI**, which
is worth sitting with.

**6. Subscriber attribution (14 total):** Apple 4 · Opus 4.6 3 · Chrome 3 · Long Context 2 ·
AI Agents Office Jobs 1 · MIT 1. The Feb-2026 batch produced 8 of 14.
*(Correction: the per-video overview panel shows "—" for subscribers; the Advanced-mode table shows
the real figures. An earlier read of "0 subscribers" was wrong.)*

**7. Retention is the second wall, and it is a BODY problem, not a hook problem.**
Opus 4.6: 14.9% avg viewed, 1:54 avg duration. 2M Tokens: 12.0%, 2:11. But YouTube's own annotation
on the Opus video reads **"51% of viewers are still watching at around the 0:30 mark, which is
typical."** The hook holds. The curve then bleeds steadily to ~10% by 6:24 and flat-lines. There is
no reversal and no escalating spine — the two things both surviving teardowns
(`WfjGZCuxl-U`, `u_5erLilDXY`) have.

**8. Shorts: 588 lifetime views — 45% of all channel views — and 83.2% swiped away, 0 subscribers,
~0.2% of watch time.** Volume without value. Shorts traffic is 90.7% Shorts-feed and does not
transfer to the long-form channel.

## What this changes

- **Stop probing for demand.** We have 22,750 impressions of evidence that the topics reach people.
- **The next test is packaging, and it is cheap and first-party.** This channel gets ~1,500–3,000
  impressions per upload with 14 subs. That is a working A/B rig for titles and thumbnails at
  near-zero risk, and it is better evidence than any external probe.
- **Runtime is not the CTR driver.** In the reliable set: 12:48→3.5%, 18:16→2.3%, 16:35→1.6%,
  7:07→1.3%, 8:39→0.5%. No relationship. Runtime belongs to the retention problem, not this one.
- **Two walls, in order: CTR 2.3% → then retention 12–15%.** Fixing the second without the first
  wastes the fix.
