## 1. The premise

**Every episode answers one question of the form "Can AI ever ___?" — and the answer is grounded in
something the next model release cannot revise.**

The positioning claim is *not* "AI is weaker than they say." That reads as cope, and it puts us in a
neighbourhood with cranks (one probe row in this lane is Vedic quantum consciousness at 1.30×).

The claim is: **the boundary is real, it is mapped, and most people cannot tell which parts of it
move.** Some limits dissolved — Go, protein folding, competition maths. Some are theorems and will
outlive everyone reading this. Telling those two apart is a genuinely hard intellectual job, and
nobody on YouTube is doing it as a beat.

That framing does four things at once:
- **It is harder than either hype or doom**, which is exactly the credibility niche.
- **It doubles the supply.** "This was supposed to be impossible and AI did it" is as much an episode
  as "this is a theorem and it never moves." A pure-negation channel runs dry and sounds bitter.
- **It generates the paradox §3 wants**, in one line: *the things AI can't do keep changing; the
  reason it can't do them hasn't changed since 1936.*
- **It inoculates against the crank neighbourhood** — a channel that regularly says "the doubters
  were wrong here" cannot be dismissed as motivated.

## 2. Why this beat and not another — the logic

**Measured (see `BEAT-CANDIDATES.md`):** PROVEN at 4.73× and 4.42× median with 69.0×/81.8× ceilings,
and — the part that matters — **three separate channels with two hits each**, all under 132k subs.
That is repeatability, not a lottery win.

**Structural, and the real argument.** The entire AI-commentary lane we already concluded we cannot
win (§0) is *date-anchored*. Every video those channels publish is dead in ninety days; they are
running on a treadmill and their library depreciates to zero. **Beat A's library appreciates.** A
video about a 1936 proof is exactly as true in 2030. That is why a 25.8k-sub publication can pull
2.11M views on a seventy-minute essay — the video kept working long after it was posted.

For a channel with **effectively no Browse/Search distribution today** (§0), a library that stays
alive is worth more than a library that decays, because the wins arrive late and then compound.

**Fit with what is already built.** Every episode is a proof, a paper, or a named researcher — so the
cream citation card carries the evidence and the dark navy panel carries the verdict, exactly as
`CONDUIT-VISUAL-SYSTEM.md` specifies. The word-synced highlight (§10.2) already exists. Nothing about
the visual system needs to change; the beat was chosen partly *because* it doesn't.

**Fit with the runtime question.** Beat A is the only measured beat where long form is evidenced:
70.2m, 62.5m, 45.2m, 31.9m, 22.8m. And structurally it satisfies §1's precondition — the verdict is
a payoff genuinely withheld across the whole runtime, not a hook that pays off at 0:20.

## 3. The spine — "THE BOUNDARY" ledger

§1 requires a persistent on-screen progress spine for anything over ~8 minutes. This beat has one for
free, and it is the same device in every episode, which is what makes it a *channel* rather than a
playlist:

```
   MOVED          — was declared impossible; is now routine
   HOLDING        — cannot do it today; no proof it never will
   PROVEN         — a theorem says never
```

The episode's claim sits on the ledger as a ghosted row (the progressive-disclosure device we already
use), migrates as evidence lands, and locks into one of the three states at the verdict. Viewers
learn the register across episodes — the ledger becomes the thing they came for.

Implementation: one-row-lit table + ghosted-slot grid. Both already in the component library.
