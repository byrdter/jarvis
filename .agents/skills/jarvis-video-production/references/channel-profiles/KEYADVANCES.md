# KeyAdvances Channel Profile

This profile governs all KeyAdvances development. It supplies channel-specific inputs to the shared
[YouTube development workflow](../WORKFLOW.md). Byrddynasty subjects, assets, taglines, presenter rules,
cadence, performance baselines, and active experiments do not carry into KeyAdvances unless Terry makes
an explicit per-video decision.

## Editorial Promise

**KeyAdvances examines specific developments changing what people can do now and in the near future.**
Each episode establishes what changed, what the evidence supports, what the limitations are, and what
the development means for human choices.

Channel tagline:

> What changes next—and what it means for you.

## Topic Universe

Search broadly across near-future developments, including:

- artificial intelligence and human-computer collaboration;
- robotics, autonomy, and advanced manufacturing;
- medicine, biotechnology, health, and longevity;
- quantum technologies and next-generation computing;
- energy, climate technology, materials, and infrastructure;
- space systems and other consequential scientific advances.

The topic must have a plausible present-to-near-future human consequence. Reject generic capability
roundups, vague futurism, daily reaction news with no durable consequence, mechanism-first tutorials,
and abstract prophecy questions.

## Topic and Demand Gate

Apply shared Gate 1 using a KeyAdvances-specific competitor set. Prefer reachable channels and recent
outliers. A candidate must answer:

1. What named, checkable development occurred?
2. What can people do, understand, avoid, or decide because of it?
3. What evidence shows viewers care about this title/subject register?
4. What is our distinct verdict or unresolved contradiction?

Probe shape families and lane demand, not exact finished titles. Read every matching row for topical
drift. Strong current candidates still fail if the successful comparison videos concern a different
audience need.

## Packaging Standard

Preferred title engine:

> **Named real thing + concrete number/fact + consequential verdict**

Use a specific, checkable subject. Lead with the verdict or human consequence rather than an internal
mechanism. Prefer a paradox or withheld meaning when it remains accurate. Avoid abstract category plus
vague question.

Thumbnail rules:

- one focal idea readable at small size;
- title and thumbnail complement rather than repeat each other;
- use one number and one noun at most when text is necessary;
- build a recognizable KeyAdvances identity: midnight navy/charcoal, restrained cyan technology light,
  warm amber human consequence, premium editorial-documentary finish;
- no generic glowing brains, robot portraits, Matrix code, neon cyberpunk cities, red arrows, interface
  clutter, or competitor imitation;
- a real human face is optional and must serve the idea; no Byrddynasty face/avatar rule applies.

The title/thumbnail package must be approved before full research, final VO, or asset generation.

## VO and Retention Standard

Use the shared information-first, evidence-dense construction:

1. Frame one carries a named document, number, artifact, event, or labeled chart.
2. The first 20 seconds establish a concrete stake or paradox.
3. By roughly 35 seconds, name a question the viewer cannot resolve alone.
4. Reveal facts freely; withhold the mechanism, meaning, twist, or verdict.
5. Compound the stake and reverse the argument at roughly 40–55%.
6. Resolve the persistent visual spine into a useful verdict.

Voice: measured, curious, precise, and human-centered. Explore rather than prophesy. Avoid hype, doom,
boilerplate greetings, biographies, and channel exposition. Technical detail earns its place by changing
what the viewer understands or can do.

Start the revival with tightly earned **8–10 minute** videos. Extend only when a whole-runtime carried
payoff, persistent visual spine, and authored reversal can sustain the additional time.

## Originality and Monetization Safety

Competitor videos may supply demand evidence and structural moves; they may not supply our wording,
research set, examples, conclusion, thumbnail composition, or visual identity. Every episode must show
original research, synthesis, judgment, or practical utility. Do not ship generic AI-written narration,
interchangeable templates, or minimally animated image slideshows.

Disclose realistic synthetic or materially altered content when required. Set the made-for-kids field
accurately according to the intended audience; it is a compliance decision, never a revenue tactic.

## Shorts Policy

**No routine Shorts and no automatic two-Shorts-per-video derivative.** KeyAdvances' existing Shorts
produced an 83.2% swipe-away rate, zero subscribers, and negligible watch time. A Short may be approved
only as an explicit experiment with its own independently compelling premise, packaging, and success
metric. Long-form revival production takes priority.

## Upload and Localization

Follow shared Gate 9: upload unlisted for processing and QA, not for algorithmic trust. Publish only
after resolution, checks, metadata, sources, language, disclosure, end-screen/card, and playback review.

Automatic dubbing is a second-stage experiment for suitable evergreen episodes. Require manual review
before publication. Check names, numbers, quotations, scientific terms, and company names. Track dubbed
language performance separately from the English release.

## Revival Baseline and Scorecard

First-party baseline from the 2026-07-31 audit:

- 22,750 lifetime impressions;
- 2.30% channel CTR;
- 40.4 lifetime watch-hours;
- 14 subscribers;
- reliable-title winner: `We Tested Anthropic's Opus 4.6 Claims — Here's What's Actually True` at
  3.5% CTR on 3,083 impressions;
- strong but smaller-sample package: `Apple Just Spent $110 Billion — Genius Move or Corporate
  Blunder?` at 7.7% CTR on 495 impressions and four subscribers;
- clearest failed package: `Will AI Agents Change Everything?` at 0.5% CTR on 2,114 impressions.

Record at 24 hours, 7 days, and 28 days when the sample supports interpretation:

| Metric | Diagnoses |
|---|---|
| Impressions | Whether YouTube found a test audience |
| CTR | Title/thumbnail and audience fit |
| First-30-second retention | Whether the concrete promise and hook held |
| Average percentage viewed | Whole-body retention independent of runtime |
| Average view duration | Minutes generated per viewer |
| Watch-hours per video | Primary channel output metric |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | Promise-to-channel conversion |
| Traffic-source mix | Browse, Suggested, Search, External, and returning behavior |

Do not diagnose from views alone. Use impressions + CTR to judge packaging, the first 30 seconds to
judge the open, the retention curve and percentage viewed to judge the body, and subscribers per 1,000
views to judge whether the episode strengthened the channel promise.

## Revival Operating Rhythm

- Publish one strong long-form video every 7–14 days initially.
- Treat the first three uploads as one measured revival batch, while keeping each video standalone.
- Do not change multiple major production variables inside the batch without documenting the test.
- After three videos, compare the batch with this baseline and update this profile only when the data
  warrants a durable rule.
