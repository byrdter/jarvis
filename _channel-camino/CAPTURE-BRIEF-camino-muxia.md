# Camino Finisterre–Muxía — capture brief
**Santiago → Ames → Negreira → A Pena → Santa Marina → A Picota → O Logoso → Cee → Fisterra → Muxía · 12 days**

Short version: **shoot video, not photos.** Ten seconds of moving footage beats ten
still shots. Phone is fine. This should cost you almost no time — if it starts
feeling like work, drop it and just do the three "zero-effort" items below.

---

## The three things that matter most (do these even if you do nothing else)

**1. Before you leave — one photo of everything laid out.**
Whole pack, contents spread on the bed or floor, shot from directly above.
Then at the end, note which items you never touched. That's it.

**2. Every day — jot what you spent.**
Just a note on your phone: `Day 3 — bed 14, dinner 12, coffee 3.50, pharmacy 6`.
Doesn't have to be exact. Doesn't have to be pretty.

**3. Every day — one voice memo, 60 seconds, out loud.**
End of the day, or while walking. Say where you are, how far you went, how your
body feels, and one thing that happened. Nobody hears these but us. Talk normally —
it does not need to be composed, and the unpolished ones are the good ones.

---

## Video: how to hold the phone

- **Horizontal.** Always horizontal.
- **Hold still and count to ten.** Longer than feels natural. Don't zoom, don't pan
  around looking for something — pick the frame, then hold it.
- **Don't narrate over the shot.** Let the wind and footsteps record. Talking goes
  in the separate voice memo.
- Shoot **into the light sometimes** — sunrise starts and long shadows are the
  best-looking material on this route.
- If you shoot the same thing twice, the second one is usually better. Keep both.

## What to shoot each day (10 seconds each, ~6 clips a day is plenty)

- **Feet and ground.** Boots on gravel, mud, cobble, sand. Down-angle, walking.
  This is the single most-used shot in every good film about this walk.
- **The waymarkers.** Yellow arrows, scallop shells, stone markers with the
  kilometer count. Especially any that are hand-painted, odd, or in a strange place.
- **The room where you slept.** Bunks, your gear on the floor, the shared kitchen,
  breakfast. Ordinary and unglamorous is exactly right.
- **Your body, honestly.** Feet at the end of a day, taping blisters, hanging
  laundry, the pack on the floor. Don't tidy up first.
- **Other walkers.** Backs of people ahead on the trail. Ask before filming faces —
  a lot of people say yes, and thirty seconds of someone explaining why they came
  is worth more than any landscape.
- **Weather.** Rain, fog, wind on the coast. Bad weather is better footage than
  good weather. Shoot it, don't wait it out.

## The specific moments on YOUR route — these are the ones we can't get later

| Where | What to get |
|---|---|
| **Santiago, leaving** | The cathedral and Praza do Obradoiro **behind you** as you walk out, in the early morning. Everyone films arriving here. Nobody films leaving. This is the most important shot of the trip — get it even if it means a slow start. |
| **Ponte Maceira** (before Negreira) | The medieval bridge over the river. From the bank, from on it, and the water underneath. Prettiest spot on the route. |
| **Negreira** | The stone archway at Pazo do Cotón you walk through. |
| **Olveiroa / Logoso** | Stone-roofed village houses, the *hórreos* (the raised stone grain sheds on legs). |
| **The fork after Hospital** | The signpost where the path splits toward Finisterre one way and Muxía the other. Hold on the sign. |
| **Coming down toward Cee** | ⚠️ **The first moment the ocean comes into view.** This is the emotional payoff of the whole walk. Start filming *before* you crest — get the reveal, not the aftermath. If you get one thing perfectly, make it this. |
| **Fisterra** | The **0.00 km marker**. The lighthouse. The bronze boot sculpture. Sunset from the point. If people are burning clothes at the end, film it from a respectful distance. |
| **Muxía, arriving** | **Santuario da Virxe da Barca** — the church sitting alone on the rocks with the sea behind it. The big flat stones (Pedra de Abalar). Waves hitting the rocks. Your own arrival — hand the phone to someone. |

## A few notes

- **Battery and space.** Video eats both. A small power bank is worth its weight,
  and clear space off your phone before you go.
- **Back up when you have wifi.** Photos/Drive, whatever's automatic. Losing the
  card is the only unrecoverable failure here.
- **Don't let this get in the way of the walk.** If a moment is worth being in
  rather than filming, be in it. There'll be plenty of others.
